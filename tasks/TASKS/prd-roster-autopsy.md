## 1. Product intent and decision status

A fantasy manager should be able to see their roster prominently in one dashboard, understand where it is strong or weak at C, LW, RW, D and G relative to the league, and find realistic ways to improve it. Recommendations must respect the league's scoring, eligible positions, roster capacity and relevant dates. A manager should be able to explore the playoff schedule and the games a trade or waiver target could actually use in their lineup.

**Owner requirements from this task:** a single dashboard; Yahoo or Fantrax roster input when supported; compact views of other league teams; position-by-position comparison using actual scoring and eligibility; useful free/manual roster and position grades without a sync; complementary, potentially mutually beneficial trade ideas with an honestly labeled one-sided fallback; playoff-period schedule and usable games for trade/waiver targets; preserve Yahoo Trade Market bundles and picks, distinguish duplicate observations, unknown context and sample bias, and attribute Yahoo with its logo. TJ reports permission for the specific Yahoo Trade Market collection subject to logo attribution. The request does not establish live coverage or a fair-market value model.

**Recommendations in this PRD:** the grade definitions, thresholds, benchmark design, candidate search, phase order, display wording and measurement targets below are concrete proposed defaults, not prior owner decisions or claims that the feature exists. Apply them to an implementation candidate after the open decisions are resolved. Existing RSO decisions remain in force for RSO; this document does not silently amend them. In particular, a category-summary heuristic here is opt-in and is not an automatic RSO category utility.

## 2. Goals, users and non-goals

**Goals**

1. A manager can enter a roster, scoring and slot rules manually and obtain useful, reproducible roster and position grades without connecting a provider or entering all other teams.
2. With sufficiently complete, comparable league rosters, the manager can see league-relative position strengths, weaknesses, peer ranks and underlying contributions; missing peers do not become an apparently complete league.
3. A manager can inspect trade ideas that show both teams' feasible before/after outcomes and the assumptions behind any potential mutual fit; a useful one-sided idea remains available when mutual benefit cannot be established.
4. A manager can select a playoff period, compare scheduled opportunities with usable lineup opportunities, and see the net effect of a trade or waiver scenario.
5. Every result carries its scoring basis, data coverage, time, source and material uncertainty. No simulated action is represented as a completed provider transaction.

**Users and stories**

- A free manager pastes their roster and rules, resolves uncertain names and positions, then sees actionable player-pool-based grades and the limitations of not having league peers.
- A connected manager selects the correct season, league and team, reviews imported rules and sees their team beside compact peer summaries.
- A manager with a C shortage and LW surplus finds a team with the opposite pattern, inspects both resulting rosters, and decides whether to approach the manager themselves.
- A categories manager inspects changes to each scored category and goalie ratio instead of receiving an unsupported single trade winner.
- A playoff-bound manager compares a target's games and open starting slots in each playoff matchup before making a trade or claim.

**Non-goals:** automatic offers, trades, waiver claims, lineup changes or messages; guaranteed acceptance, standings gain or playoff victory; a new FORGE model, replacement RSO optimizer or second BLSH interpretation system; ownership percentage as a trade price; keeper/pick valuation or salary/cap legality invented from incomplete rules; unbounded scraping; an assertion that a sparse sample establishes fair market value. This planning document makes no database, production, credential, deployment, Git or external-communication change.

## 3. Dashboard and setup requirements

- Place the user's roster and overall grade in the prominent primary panel. Show compact peer team rows/cards with C/LW/RW/D/G grade or status, sortable by team and expandable into player/slot details. Selecting a position filters the comparison and explains the user's strengths and gaps; selecting a peer opens the relevant roster and possible trade fits. Preserve hierarchy on desktop and make roster, league comparison, trade ideas and playoff mode accessible as focused mobile views without requiring whole-page horizontal scrolling. Use text/icons as well as color, visible focus and keyboard/touch controls.
- Start with **Manual**, **Connect Yahoo**, and **Connect Fantrax** paths where entitlement and provider capability permit. Manual is available without a provider account or paid sync. Present current capability per provider; until Fantrax current-roster and availability feeds are qualified, offer reviewed manual roster entry and existing settings import where available, not a misleading connected-roster promise.
- Scope the workspace to season, league, team, timezone and chosen analysis window. Confirm the owner/team selection; never substitute another account or league. Display last source observation per roster, rules, stats and schedule. Refresh is explicit for manual inputs; connected refresh uses bounded existing provider access and stops/backoffs on access errors or rate limits.
- Allow search and paste of player names; show identity candidates, require resolution for ambiguous/unmatched entries, and let the manager correct player/team/eligible positions and enter bench, IR and other roster status. Flag duplicate players and unresolved identity instead of merging names or silently dropping rows. Manual eligible positions are manager-reported, not provider-verified. A player catalog position alone is not proof of fantasy eligibility.
- Collect or review scoring format, every relevant stat/weight or category and direction, roster slot counts and allowed positions, lineup lock mode, timezone and league/team count when known. Permit editing and identify provider-reported, manager-supplied, inferred and unknown facts. No default preset may be called the league's actual settings until the manager confirms it. Allow optional peer rosters to be pasted/edited in manual mode, with dates and ownership marked manager-reported.
- Save one versioned manual workspace locally on the same browser/device, with edit, reset/export and a clear local-storage notice. The app must not require all opponents' rosters for the manual grade. Degraded or expired connected access retains manual continuation and clearly labeled last-known inputs. Never overwrite edits on refresh without an explicit reconciliation choice.

### FR2 — Coverage before comparison

For each displayed result, show the basis (peer league or public player pool), scoring and period, source and as-of time, covered/expected teams, players and scored categories, unresolved entries, plus a fresh/stale/partial/unavailable state and reason. Eligibility, ownership, league rules, schedule and player stats have independent freshness; a new fetch does not refresh an older forecast issuance. A partial league cohort may be inspected with its `n / expected` coverage, but must not be labeled a full-league rank; if team count is unknown, say so. An absent player, page, forecast, or provider response is unknown, not an empty roster, a free agent, zero output or last place.

## 4. Grading and league-scoring requirements

### FR3 — Scoring and common basis

1. Normalize known provider and manual categories to canonical stat IDs with a versioned mapping. Preserve source names, units, modifiers, category direction and unknown fields. Points values are `sum(actual league weight × supported stat)` including a penalty or bonus only if the rules explicitly score it. Do not double-count overlapping categories such as PPP or apply a participation probability twice. Unsupported nonlinear bonuses or unrecognized scored categories block a complete points grade; show supported components as partial.
2. Category leagues retain the complete vector of the actual scored categories, direction and scoring format. Compare higher/lower correctly. Calculate SV% from total saves / total shots against and GAA from `60 × total goals against / goalie minutes`; zero or unknown denominators are unavailable. Report the numerator, denominator and sample exposure; never average player or daily ratios. A ratio of expected counts is identified as such, not an expected ratio or category-win probability.
3. Use one declared, identical basis for every compared team: same season, cutoff, date range or per-appearance window, category definitions, forecast version/conditioning, eligibility rules and unit. Explicitly disclose each team's excluded players and missing heads. Do not compare a historical per-appearance rate against a projected playoff total or a next-10-games package against a common calendar period as if equivalent.
4. Offer qualified common-period forecasts when their actual allowed uses and coverage support the comparison. If unavailable, use a separately labeled **historical player-pool benchmark**, based on actual past box-score statistics and actual supplied scoring; it is descriptive, not a forecast. Proposed default is each player's latest ten recorded appearances ending at the cutoff, with a minimum of five and a visible smaller-sample/unavailable state. A manager may select another supported common window; the sample threshold and universe require review. Season start, missed games or unavailable categories may leave some grades partial. No qualifying stat dataset means an explicit unavailable result and a way to correct inputs, not fabricated grades.

### FR4 — Eligible positions, flex and double counting

- Normalize the league's actual `C`, `LW`, `RW`, `D`, `G` and supported `F`, `W`, `UTIL`/other flex slot eligibility into concrete slot instances. Keep bench and reserves distinct from active slots and honor league-specific restrictions. A multi-position player is eligible for each supplied position, but occupies **at most one slot per day** and contributes at most once to any simultaneous roster or trade total. Locks, reserve designations and effective dates restrict that assignment.
- Use the same feasible slot allocation rules for every peer and both sides of a trade. With equal supported values, use a stable tie-break based on slot ID and canonical player ID. Report assigned slot/position and unfilled slots; distinguish a confirmed empty slot from an unknown player/position.
- For position cards, show the primary assigned contribution and, where useful, a clearly labeled **alternative-eligibility view** for the same C/LW player in another feasible position. Alternative views are separate scenarios and may not be added together. In leagues using only F/W/UTIL, show C/LW/RW depth as eligible-position scenarios and explain flex use rather than inventing dedicated slots; show `N/A — no usable slot` for a position with no allowed slot.
- RSO's local matcher is a reuse candidate, subject to its actual contract and tests. Its inspected code converts a non-finite value to zero for attribution: the autopsy adapter must reject or explicitly exclude missing-value inputs before matching, so this fallback never becomes a grade or a claim of no contribution.

### FR5 — Concrete grade proposal

The product must deliver both a roster grade and position grades in free/manual mode with a complete supported roster and rules, even with no peer data. The following is a reviewable starting algorithm, not an approved calibration:

1. Build a versioned public reference pool from the same sport/season, cutoff, stat basis and scoring rules. Deduplicate canonical player IDs. Use provider eligibility when it is available for that context; otherwise use a documented position proxy in the pool and label the comparison approximate. The user's own eligibility remains reviewed manual or provider evidence. Show the pool size and age; apply the same inclusion and minimum-exposure rules to the roster and pool. A pool with fewer than ten qualified players for a position is insufficient under this proposed default.
2. For points, compute each qualified player's supported points per appearance on the selected historical basis, or their admitted contribution on the declared forecast basis, then convert it to a position-pool percentile. For categories, compute a percentile for each applicable category against the eligible position's same-basis pool. Before a composite is selected, show category-specific grades and their independently optimized legal assignments as explicitly alternative scenarios; do not add those lineups together or claim one simultaneous overall category result. A composite category grade is available only after the manager explicitly accepts an equal-category heuristic or supplies nonnegative priorities: normalize the selected applicable category weights to sum to one and use their weighted mean of player category percentiles as that player's scalar slot score. Show league-defined category weights separately. This heuristic describes the chosen comparison, not a win probability or a universally optimal category blend.
3. Calculate each position-pool percentile as `100 × (number strictly below + 0.5 × number tied) / pool size`, reversing comparison for lower-is-better categories; include the player once in the comparison pool. For points or an explicitly selected category composite, choose the legal unique assignment across all active slots maximizing the sum of the resulting scalar slot scores. For a flex slot use the chosen eligible position's pool and retain that attribution. Do not percentile-rank the category composite a second time. Deterministically break ties. Show assigned players, their scores and relevant scored contributions, plus bench depth separately. A confirmed vacant slot contributes zero to the **slot score**; an unknown eligibility/value is never a vacant slot.
4. The overall score is the mean across all active slot instances of that single legal assignment, including verified vacancies. A position with dedicated slots uses the mean of the assigned percentiles for those exact slots; show flex assignments separately so every team uses the same slot count in that position comparison. If the league has no dedicated slot for a position but has flex slots that accept it, calculate a separately labeled, non-additive **conditional depth** grade: assign at most one eligible player to each slot that accepts that position, use that fixed slot count as the denominator for every team, and explain that this scenario allocates those flex slots to that position. It cannot be added to the overall score or other position scenarios. Convert scores to proposed descriptive letters: A `>= 80`, B `>= 60`, C `>= 40`, D `>= 20`, F `< 20`; show the score and method next to the letter. These grade the roster against a **player pool**, not its league rank, championship odds, or a calibrated forecast. Do not present an unqualified complete letter when unknown players, slots or scored categories could change the assignment. Instead show a provisional covered subset, exclusions and how to complete it, or `Unavailable` if the subset is not meaningful.
5. For complete peer rosters use the same basis and assignment for every team. Show each team's slot score and a separate within-league rank, with midranks for ties; higher score ranks first. Position cards must expose applicable actual categories and any differences in per-team coverage. Proposed default: a league-relative rank needs at least four complete comparable teams; with fewer, show their named values and `insufficient peers` rather than a league percentile. A missing peer means a **partial cohort** rank only, never `#N in the league`. Show the distribution, last updated time and a position's strongest/weakest contributing players or categories.

The roster grade's slot-average is intentionally a simple explanatory benchmark. It does not incorporate future injury, trading intent, bench replacement value or daily schedule congestion; those are separate contexts and are not hidden inside the letter. Position scenario cards for flex players are not additive to the overall score. In category mode, a mean of player percentile scores is a heuristic index, not a team SV% or GAA; separately calculate and display any team-level category ratios from the complete roster numerators and denominators as in FR3.

**Assignment example:** with one C slot and one LW slot, player P is C/LW with pool percentiles C 90 and LW 80, and player Q is C-only at 70. The legal maximum is Q at C and P at LW: C `70` (B), LW `80` (A), overall `(70 + 80) / 2 = 75` (B). Assigning P at C as well would count that player twice. This is a deterministic illustrative fixture, not a real player's rating.

## 5. Trade ideas and market evidence

### FR6 — Construct realistic candidates

Generate an advisory shortlist using known current rosters: identify the user's weaker and stronger eligible positions/categories and look for peers with complementary weaknesses and surpluses. The first bounded search should include one-for-one and small two-player packages; allow the manager to edit either side, exclude players and protect players. Disclose search size/caps and label results **best found**, not globally optimal. Never imply a player is owned by a particular team without current scoped evidence or a manager-reported scenario. Check known trade deadlines and processing delays when evaluating effective dates. When roster status, trade restrictions, deadline, keeper/pick ownership, salary/cap, eligibility, lock or effective timing are unknown, mark the affected idea conditional or exclude it from the actionable shortlist; do not assert legality.

For each team independently, compare the optimized original roster against the optimized roster after the entire proposed exchange over the same dates, scoring, eligible slots, evidence, manager locks and rules. Include player departures, added players, flex reshuffling, displaced starters, capacity, necessary drops/adds and their costs. Uneven packages require an explicit legal capacity resolution or an incomplete/conditional result. Preserve manager protections and do not invent a replacement player or free capacity. Show `before → after`, net active-game opportunities and, when supported, projected points or category vectors for **both** teams, with assumptions and downside. Match each comparison's objective: the maximum-starts baseline and after-plan compare with each other; any scoring-optimal baseline and after-plan compare with each other. If they select different lineups, show the tradeoff rather than mixing their totals. Raw player/package projections may be shown separately but cannot be called roster-aware gains.

For a points league, a **Potential mutual fit** requires positive, complete net startable points for both teams under the same supported scenario and a visible practical tolerance selected in the method. For example, if Team A gives up 8 startable points and receives 12, and Team B gives up 9 and receives 11 after their respective full-roster re-optimizations, show A `+4` and B `+2`; those numbers describe the scenario, not an acceptance probability. If player uncertainty or model coverage makes the difference indistinguishable, show `close / uncertain`, not an assured benefit. No numeric confidence, interval, counterparty preference, fairness or acceptance probability may be invented.

For a categories league, first show each team's before/after vector. A conservative potential mutual fit requires each side to improve at least one relevant category and worsen none beyond an explicitly declared tolerance, with all affected categories supported. Mixed-sign results are **tradeoffs**, unless the manager selects a transparent decision rule; any inferred counterparty priorities are assumptions, not facts. Protect lower-is-better categories and recompute goalie ratios from each roster's before/after count/minute totals. The product must not add category percentiles to claim an automatic winner.

### FR7 — Fallback and action language

If no two-sided match survives the bounded search, or peer data and rules are incomplete, still show useful **one-sided ideas** based on the user's supported improvement: e.g. a target, why their eligible position helps, who would be displaced, a possible give-up when known, and what would need checking. If the target's owner or availability is unknown, say `owner/availability unverified`; do not invent a partner or label the idea mutual. Distinguish `no match found in searched candidates` from proof no match exists, and distinguish no improvement from insufficient data. The manager acts in Yahoo/Fantrax; there is no submit, contact or roster-write action.

Retain source URL/type, capture and source date text, observation time/timezone if known, parse version and completeness. Unknown league scoring, size, keeper format, motivations, trade timing and relationship to the current user's league remain unknown. A pick may not be dropped simply to produce a one-for-one comparison.

Link sightings as a single deal only when a provider-scoped stable identifier and its scope are verified. Otherwise retain each occurrence and an optional labeled package-similarity cluster; neither sighting nor cluster count is a verified unique-trade count. Replaying identical capture + parser version is idempotent; reparsing appends a version. Changed evidence remains visible. Quarantine ambiguous or malformed records without losing raw evidence; an error, challenge, 429, missing table or unverified empty marker is not `no trades`.
- Present an observed package as an example with date/source, complete sides, sample count and unknown context. Disclose collection scope, sampled player selection, coverage and survivor/selection bias. Sparse or repeated observations cannot support a verified fair-market price, suggested equal-value ratio, calibrated acceptance probability or a population-wide claim. Do not rank trade recommendations by an unvalidated market value. Any future aggregate or value model needs separately reviewed rights, deduplication, representative coverage, holdout evaluation and comparable league context.

Display the Yahoo logo and source attribution on Yahoo-derived surfaces, including relevant exported views if export is permitted. Do not use Yahoo labeling for synthetic or unrelated data.

## 6. Playoff mode and usable games

### FR9 — Date periods and schedule

Provide a Playoff mode with the league's verified playoff matchup periods where available, otherwise let the manager enter and label inclusive date ranges and timezone. Show each period's start/end and source; support combined, irregular or multi-week rounds, custom ranges and a season crossing where supported. The manager can choose regular-season or playoff analysis independently of today's date. Schedule data must match the NHL season, team and game identity; record observation/revision time and handle postponements, cancellations, reschedules and unavailable future schedules without double counting a game.

For each roster, trade target or waiver target, show per playoff period: team schedule with opponent and date, **scheduled opportunities** (team has a game), **usable active-game opportunities** (a legal assignment exists in the proposed roster on that day) and bench/congestion/lock/reserve/eligibility/timing exclusions. Scheduled games are not a guarantee of player participation or a goalie start. A player acquired later cannot contribute before the effective instant. Confirmed, expected, unknown and stale goalie-start evidence remain distinct; do not count two competing goalies as two confirmed starts for one team-game.

Calculate target impact by comparing the complete hypothetical roster, including an explicit drop if needed, with the same no-move optimized baseline. Show both gross target opportunities and **net** usable games and, where qualified, net scored contribution after displaced starts and the outgoing player. Compare maximum-starts lineups with maximum-starts baselines and scoring-optimal lineups with scoring-optimal baselines; show both when they differ. A free agent/waiver label requires current league-scoped evidence or an explicit manager verification; otherwise say `availability unknown`. Waiver clearance, acquisition budgets, locks and roster capacity can make an idea conditional. The manager may inspect the schedule even when the trade/waiver is not yet verified.

For weekly-lock leagues, apply a qualified weekly lock and whole-period roster constraints; if that is unsupported or unknown, show schedule only and withhold claims of legal usable games. If forecasts are unavailable, show usable games when rules and eligibility suffice, and mark expected points/categories unavailable. Never equate GP with startable gain.

## 9. Edge cases and acceptance criteria

Acceptance requires deterministic fixtures with independent expected calculations and a user-visible end-to-end path; a mock alone does not establish live provider coverage. Proposed gates:

| Case | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Free/manual only | With no provider account or peer roster, a manager enters a complete points league and roster, confirms eligibility, and receives a roster grade and C/LW/RW/D/G grades or explained `N/A` for absent slot classes. Pool, date, actual weights, number of players and limitations are visible. Reload retains the one local workspace; editing a weight changes the calculated result. |
| Category manual | A category roster shows per-category grades, including lower-is-better and goalie ratios. A composite appears only after explicit heuristic/priorities selection. No supported goalie denominator means unavailable, not zero. |

Unknown category, nonfinite input, incompatible horizon, expired/denied forecast and mixed conditionality block the affected complete result instead of becoming zero.

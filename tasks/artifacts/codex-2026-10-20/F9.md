## Descriptive contract v1

Population: caller supplies a complete, deduplicated regular-season result population and the complete schedule population being described (which may be focal-team fixtures). No implicit 82-game denominator, priors, venue adjustment or team-performance forecast. Source qualification/completeness is a caller prerequisite, not established by a non-null arithmetic result.

Each fixture and result has stable ID, participating teams, start timestamp and schedule availability timestamp. Results additionally have completion timestamp, result availability timestamp, winner and OT flag. Clock inputs require explicit ISO timestamps with timezone; date-only or timezone-free availability is rejected. Results must be completed and known **strictly before** the record cutoff; equality is excluded. Availability must not precede completion. Duplicate fixture/result IDs, impossible calendar dates and invalid result chronology fail rather than double-count. If a result ID matches a supplied fixture, its team pair and start instant must agree (team order may differ); other historical result IDs need not appear in the described fixture list. Caller must resolve schedule revisions and certify stable IDs/source population before invocation; this function cannot detect a source assigning different IDs to the same real game.

Views:

- `past-as-known-then`: scheduled fixtures starting before the requested cutoff; evaluate each opponent using results available strictly before that fixture's start. The fixture itself must have been known before its start.
- `past-retrospective`: scheduled fixtures starting before cutoff; evaluate all at the requested cutoff. Later results/corrections known before cutoff can affect earlier schedule difficulty.
- `future`: scheduled fixtures starting at/after cutoff that were known strictly before cutoff; evaluate opposition using historical results known before cutoff. Indirect quality uses historical opponents, never opponents' remaining schedules.

The fixture count describes this supplied schedule population; it is not a claim that every started fixture was completed or a player-usable start. Completed-game volume, schedule revisions, postponed/canceled fixture handling, and roster usability need their own qualified source/status rules. No hardcoded season game count is used.

For a record with wins W, regulation losses L and overtime losses O, N=W+L+O:

- win% = W/N.
- points% = (2W+O)/(2N).
- N=0, a missing component, negative/noninteger/nonfinite counts => null. W=0 with positive N => observed zero.

For focal team i, remove **all** games involving i before evaluating its opponents. For each scheduled meeting with j, compute j's eligible record percentage, then average those percentages over meetings: repeated meetings count repeatedly, but j's history length is its own record denominator. Example: B's 1–1 record twice and C's 3–0 once give (0.5+0.5+1)/3=2/3, not pooled 5/7 or unique-opponent 3/4.

Indirect term: for each meeting with j, consider j's eligible historical meetings with k after removing focal i games. Evaluate k's record after removing both i and j games, average across those meetings, then across i's fixtures. Removing j as well is an explicit versioned anti-feedback choice; this is an RPI-style variant, not an assertion of universal NCAA/NHL conventions. Direct and indirect components are reported separately, with no automatic blend or team-strength label.

No partial renormalization: a component is null if any selected fixture lacks its required record support. Report selected fixture count, supported direct/indirect fixture counts, per-fixture record cutoff, opponent history count and values. Empty/all-unsupported/disconnected support stays explicit. There is no neutral-zero imputation.

## Offline benchmark protocol and formulas

No qualified chronological NHL source packet was supplied or admitted. All fixtures are generated locally from fixed latent values `[1.2,0.8,0.3,-0.3,-0.8,-1.2]`, xorshift seed `20261020`, with separate chronological train/test clocks. G is isolated. Outcomes are binary draws with sigmoid latent difference except the deliberate complete-separation training stress case; every fifth generated fixture is marked OT solely to characterize points% sensitivity. No goal counts, offense/defense categories, injuries, lineups, market or decision-value data are modeled.

Compared methods:

1. OWP-only: meeting-weighted opponents' win%, excluding focal games.
2. Descriptive SOS 2:1 blend `(2*OWP+OOWP)/3` as a deliberately unqualified strength comparator.
3. RPI-style strength `WP/4 + OWP/2 + OOWP/4`, separately with win% and points%. Same focal/second-hop exclusions as above; missing support remains null.
4. Regularized Bradley–Terry: minimize binary negative log likelihood plus `sum(theta_i^2)/2` (fixed lambda=1); `P(i beats j)=sigmoid(theta_i-theta_j)`. Deterministic gradient descent, at most 20,000 steps, max absolute gradient <1e-10. Ridge makes separation/isolated components finite; isolated theta=0 is an explicit prior, not evidence of neutral ability.
5. Colley: solve `C*r=b`, `C_ii=2+n_i`, `C_ij=-n_ij`, `b_i=1+(wins_i-losses_i)/2`. Binary winners only; OT loser points are not counted in this comparator. Bound to at most 32 teams/2,000 games. Require residual <1e-10 and ratings within [0,1]; isolated r=0.5 is a prior.

BT uses its model probability. Bounded heuristic/Colley ratings use the **predeclared, uncalibrated** mapping `sigmoid(4*(r_i-r_j))`. This scale choice materially affects Brier/log loss and prevents claims of method superiority from these numbers. No hyperparameter, scale, blend, or points policy is tuned against test results.

Output includes native method support, same-pair common support, Brier, log loss, generator-probability RMSE, rating components, graph components, solver diagnostics, and assumptions. Cross-component predictions are unavailable for every method; regularization does not establish relative disconnected strength. A disconnected three-team component has OWP support but no OOWP support after the declared exclusions, so common support across *all* methods is empty. Do not compare unequal-support averages as if paired.

Primary formula references: [Bradley–Terry probability model, UCLA](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/logistic.pdf); [Gaussian-prior paired-comparison estimation research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08644); [Colley's original matrix derivation](https://www.colleyrankings.com/matrate.pdf). Ridge/step size, exclusions, fixture weights, synthetic seed and probability maps are declared experimental choices here.

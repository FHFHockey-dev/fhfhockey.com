# Proven NHL methods expose FORGE's remaining gaps

The proven way to project one NHL game is **opportunity times a regressed rate**. Allocate each team's ice time by strength state inside a fixed budget, then multiply by per-60 rates pulled toward position- and role-specific priors in proportion to sample size: lightly for shot, hit and block volume, heavily for shooting percentage, point share and save percentage. Build goals as shots × regressed finishing, assists as on-ice goals × point share, and goalie value from start probability, shots against and team win odds rather than recent save percentage. Wrap each mean in a count distribution (Poisson for goals; negative binomial for overdispersed shots, hits, blocks and saves), sum the weighted means for fantasy points, and use de-vigged sportsbook prices as priors and calibration targets compared in probability space, never as a mean against a line. Judge everything with bias, RMSE and proper scoring rules, binned by forecast tier, on a point-in-time rolling origin against naive and market baselines; MAE alone rewards forecasting zero. FORGE already has this skeleton and an evaluation harness more rigorous than anything a public provider documents. Its measured under-projection (**−0.44 fantasy points per player-game, top decile about 19% low**) came from plumbing, not talent estimation, and the pending history fix cuts the bias to −0.16. The remaining gaps are structural: assists are derived from the player's own goals, market prices are stored but only used for mean-versus-line flags, uncertainty is Poisson-only, league priors are hard-coded, save percentage is regressed far less than the evidence supports, and hits and blocks ignore rink bias.

## Opportunity times a regressed rate is the consensus skater recipe

Every serious public system, from Evolving-Hockey's projections to RotoWire's goal-scorer model and Daily Faceoff's DFS tables, estimates per-minute rates and multiplies them by projected minutes. None projects points per game directly. Evolving-Hockey found it "often better and more reliable to model the underlying raw metrics" than ratios, and that prior-year values of each metric were the dominant predictors ([Evolving-Hockey](https://evolving-hockey.com/?p=708)). The leading player-rating models add the second half of the recipe: Evolving-Hockey's RAPM is a TOI-weighted ridge regression ([Evolving-Hockey](https://evolving-hockey.com/glossary/regularized-adjusted-plus-minus/)), and HockeyViz's Magnus 9 shrinks each season toward last season's estimate plus an aging term ([HockeyViz](https://hockeyviz.com/txt/magnus9EV)). Both pull small samples toward a prior.

### Ice time comes first and must fit a team budget

Ice time is both the most stable and the most leveraged input. Year-to-year TOI/GP R² runs **about 0.74** (0.759 for players with 10+ GP in both seasons). Role jumps still happen: risers gain 1.3–1.8 minutes on average, and a few defencemen gained 5–9 minutes in one season ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=89836)). Deployment follows a depth chart, where each rank plays about a minute less than the one above and #1 forwards average about 21 minutes ([HockeyViz](https://hockeyviz.com/txt/magnus9EV)). Top defencemen are capped near **41–45% of team even-strength time** ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=89786)). The leverage is large: moving a 10-shots/60 player from 15:36 to 18:00 raises his chance of clearing 2.5 shots from **48.2% to 57.7%** ([BettorEdge](https://www.bettoredge.com/post/nhl-player-props-explained)). A per-game model should therefore fill seats, not project players independently. At 5v5, forwards' shares must sum to three seats and defencemen's to two, or a scratch silently inflates the team total.

The power play is where projections break. Teams averaged 2.9–3.1 PP opportunities per game for a decade until 2024-25 fell by **0.31 per team-game**, to about 2.7, a record low. Meanwhile top-unit skaters' share of PP time rose from 67.6% to 70.1% ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=89786); [newsio](https://newsio.com/?p=172319)). Team PP1 shares range from about 55% to 80%, so a league-wide PP1 constant misfits both extremes ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=85576); [DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=85204)). Dobber's audit of 285 forwards shows the cost of carrying an old environment forward. Points were overshot by **6.5%** and goals by **11.4%**, and the PP explained over half of the points gap. The top 45 projected goal scorers finished more than six goals per 82 games short, and forward shots came in at 1.99 per game against 2.17 projected ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=88967)). The defensible recipe has three steps. First, project team PP opportunities from both teams' penalty rates, regressed to the current-season league mean. Second, multiply by minutes per opportunity. Third, split by team-specific unit shares, overridden by the latest confirmed PP units.

### Volume stabilizes quickly; finishing and point share barely move

Hockey's documented shrinkage practice is Marcel-style: weight three or four seasons (5/4/3 in baseball's original) and pad with league-average "phantom" sample ([Hockey-Graphs](https://hockey-graphs.com/2014/02/03/forecasting-future-goalie-performance-with-four-year-hockey-marcels/)). In empirical-Bayes form, projected rate = (Σ wᵢ·eventsᵢ + k·prior) / (Σ wᵢ·opportunitiesᵢ + k). The reliability n/(n + k) then sets how fast current data takes over. No public source publishes k for individual skater shots/60, shooting %, IPP, hits/60 or blocks/60, so k has to be fitted in-house, by split-half correlation or by gamma-Poisson and beta-binomial maximum likelihood.

The direction is clear even without published values. Volume behaves like habit and needs small k. Percentages regress hard: Tulsky's 11% on-ice shooters projected to about 9.2% over the next three seasons against a 7.5% league mean, roughly **51% regression** ([TSN](https://www.tsn.ca/examining-on-ice-shooting-percentage-by-position-1.338499)), and even Stamkos and Ovechkin fell about two points from their starting shooting % ([TSN](https://www.tsn.ca/a-cautionary-tale-about-shooting-performance-1.894187?tsn-amp)). Individual point percentage averages about **68–70% for forwards and 30% for defencemen**. Its repeatability is disputed: Yost calls it "not a repeatable skill", while Dobber finds elite forwards hold near 80% ([Russian Machine Never Breaks](https://russianmachineneverbreaks.com/2015/09/16/what-individual-point-percentage-can-tell-us-about-the-2015-16-caps/); [DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=81332)).

The prior's centre matters as much as its strength. Regressing everyone to one league mean is the classic way to over-shrink stars. FanGraphs contrasts Marcel's single mean with OOPSY's profile-specific means ([SI/FanGraphs](https://www.si.com/mlb/2007/02/19/fangraphs-projections)), and Magnus re-centres forwards and defencemen separately ([HockeyViz](https://hockeyviz.com/txt/magnus9EV)). Magnus also supplies a ready template for age: a quadratic aging term around 24, prior strength that falls from 50,000 at age 24 to 5,000 at 18 and 30, and new players entered at **−10% of league offence** rather than at average ([HockeyViz](https://hockeyviz.com/txt/magnus9EV)). Aging curves put the scoring peak between 24 and 28, with a mild decline from about 29. Delta-method curves understate late-career decline because weaker players drop out ([Hockey-Graphs](https://hockey-graphs.com/2017/03/23/a-new-look-at-aging-curves-for-nhl-skaters-part-1/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14017)). Early-season blending follows from the same formula. A Luszczyszyn-inspired model gives the current season 0% weight on day one and only about 50% after a full year ([NL Ice Data](https://nlicedata.substack.com/p/assessing-players-teams-and-projecting)). Role changes (TOI, PP unit, linemates) are different: they are information, and should update opportunity immediately.

### Goals, assists and power-play points decompose by strength state

Shots are more consistent than goals and better predictors of future goals ([arXiv 1205.1746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.1746)). MoneyPuck keeps finishing talent as a separate Bayesian estimate of goals versus xG ([MoneyPuck](https://moneypuck.com/about.htm)). The best-supported goal model is therefore Σ over EV, PP and SH of TOI × shots/60 × regressed shooting %, or ixG/60 × a finishing multiplier shrunk toward 1.0. Its baselines must be current. League goals per team-game moved from 2.98 to 3.08 between 2018-19 and 2025-26 while shots against fell from **31.3 to 27.6** ([Hockey-Reference](https://www.hockey-reference.com/leagues/stats.html)). That implies goals per shot rose from roughly 9.5% to 11%. A shot prior anchored before 2024 over-projects volume, and a shooting-% prior anchored there under-projects finishing.

Assists belong to the line, not the shooter. The structure the sources support is TOI × on-ice goals-for/60 × (IPP − individual goal share), by strength state. On-ice goals come from a context-adjusted, regressed rate such as RAPM xGF/GF ([Evolving-Hockey](https://evolving-hockey.com/glossary/regularized-adjusted-plus-minus/)), and IPP is shrunk toward forward and defence priors. Points then follow from on-ice goals × point involvement; a player earns at most one point per goal, so modelling goals and assists as independent Poissons overstates points variance. Power-play points are the same calculation on PP minutes, PP on-ice goals and PP IPP, and PP goals carry more two-assist goals than even strength ([Japers' Rink](https://www.japersrink.com/primary-thoughts-on-secondary-02-15-2019/)). The commonly quoted ratio of about 1.7 assists per goal is consistent with 70%/30% IPP baselines but is not sourced, so compute it from your own season totals.

### Hits and blocks need rink correction and wide tails

Scorekeepers distort hits and blocks more than any other counting stat. Schuckers and Macdonald's 2007-13 rink effects ran from **0.59 (NJ) to 1.30 (LA) for hits** and **0.54 (NJ) to 1.27 (MTL) for blocks**. Shots on goal showed almost no persistent effect. Adjustment moved Clutterbuck's 2012-13 hits from 155 to about 181 ([arXiv 1412.1035](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1412.1035)). The method is to divide each event by its rink's multiplier to get a neutral rate, then multiply by tonight's rink multiplier and any homer term. Knodell's 2025 revisit adopted a home adjustment of 0.962 and a 1.03-per-goal score effect for hits. He also found the rink spread narrowed sharply in 2023-24, possibly because of tracking. Old multipliers should therefore be re-estimated on recent seasons and shrunk toward 1.0 ([Puck Over the Glass](https://puckovertheglass.substack.com/p/revisiting-scorekeeper-bias-and-scorevenue)). Teams without the puck hit and block more, so projected possession is a sensible covariate alongside player habit.

### Count distributions turn means into probabilities

Team goals per game have been modelled as Poisson since the 1970s ([Buttrey, Washburn & Price](https://faculty.nps.edu/awashburn/docs/EstimatingNHLScoringRates.pdf)). No published NHL study fits player-level per-game distributions, but the theory is settled. A Poisson whose rate varies from game to game (TOI, score state, opponent) or is uncertain (a gamma posterior from shrinkage) is exactly a negative binomial, with Var = μ + αμ² ([John D. Cook](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2009/09/22/negative-binomial-distribution/)). Goals, with means around 0.1–0.5, stay close to Poisson because αμ² is tiny. Shots, hits, blocks and saves are materially overdispersed, so Poisson overstates P(≥1) and understates both tails. Practitioners agree that real shot totals are "a little more spread out than a pure Poisson" ([BettorEdge](https://www.bettoredge.com/post/nhl-player-props-explained)). Dispersion α per stat and position has to be fitted on game logs, and explicit TOI modelling handles most of what zero-inflation would.

### Context adjustments should be small and owned once

Teammates matter far more than opponents: Magnus finds the teammate distribution "much bigger" than the competition distribution ([HockeyViz](https://hockeyviz.com/txt/magnus9EV)), and over a season competition "for the most part evens out" ([Hockey-Graphs](https://hockey-graphs.com/2014/01/01/overemphasizing-context-a-mistake-just-as-poor-as-explaining-context-in-the-first-place/)). The measurable game-level effects are modest. Home teams win about 54%, outscore opponents by **about +0.27 goals per game** and generate 42.6 against 40.7 unblocked 5v5 attempts per 60, which works out to roughly ±4% per team ([nhlscraper](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/nhlscraper/vignettes/back-to-back-tax.html); [Meghan Hall](https://meghan.rbind.io/blog/2020-08-03-last-change/)). Zero rest costs about 0.2 goals of differential at the same venue, roughly 3–4% of a team's scoring if split evenly ([nhlscraper](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/nhlscraper/vignettes/back-to-back-tax.html)), and Magnus calls fatigue "incredibly tiny" relative to other effects ([HockeyViz](https://hockeyviz.com/txt/magnus9EV)). Score effects reshape shot volume far more than scoring: Evolving-Hockey's 5v5 shot weights run from 0.862 to 1.191 across score states but only ±5–9% for xG ([Evolving-Hockey](https://evolving-hockey.com/glossary/score-adjustments/)), so favourites' shots should be shaded down and underdogs' up. Opponent and goalie multipliers deserve restraint, and RotoWire's production goal-scorer model damps both **50% toward neutral** ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/hockey/article/nhl-anytime-goal-scorer-model-137385)). Game outcomes are only so predictable. MoneyPuck's favourites win just 60–64%, with log loss of 0.648–0.661 ([MoneyPuck](https://moneypuck.com/about.htm)). That ceiling argues for small, single-owner context terms.

| Stat | Proven construction | Regression strength | Distribution | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goals | Σ TOI × SOG/60 × regressed Sh% by strength (or ixG × finishing → 1.0) | Volume light, Sh% heavy | Poisson adequate | Raw recent goal rates; stale league Sh% |
| Assists | Σ TOI × on-ice GF/60 × (IPP − goal share) | IPP and on-ice Sh% heavy | Poisson/NB, correlated with linemates | Tying assists to the player's own goals |
| Points | On-ice goals × point involvement | As above | Team-simulated | Independent G and A draws |
| PPP | Team PP opportunities × min/opportunity × unit share × PP on-ice GF × PP IPP | Unit share from latest lineup | Poisson/NB | Prior PP environment carried forward |
| SOG | Σ TOI × SOG/60 × home/score/opponent | Light (habit) | Negative binomial | Poisson tails; pre-2024 shot priors |
| Hits, blocks | Rink-neutral rate × tonight's rink effect × TOI × possession | Light, after scorer correction | Negative binomial | Raw rates; 2007-13 multipliers |

## Goalie projections hinge on start odds and shot volume, not save percentage

Start probability comes first, and workload is spreading. The goalie with the most starts on his team took **69.3% of team games in 2012 but 58.2% in 2023**, or 47.7 starts ([Robnett & Welsh, CMU](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/sure/2023/showcase/hockey_saves/report.html)), and coaches now "typically split starts" on back-to-backs ([NHL.com](https://nhl.com/kraken/news/core-concepts-examining-goalie-workload-329507592)). MoneyPuck runs a separate start-probability model using rest, recent results, playing time, age and two-year GSAx, and switches to the confirmed starter when media report one ([MoneyPuck](https://moneypuck.com/about.htm)). Lineup services label goalies confirmed, expected or unconfirmed, but no one publishes how often "likely" calls hold. A projection should therefore fit its own mapping from label tier and hours-to-puck-drop to P(start), and report E[FP] = P(start) × E[FP | start] + P(relief) × E[FP | relief].

Saves and goals against should be built as **projected shots against × a heavily regressed save %**. Save % reaches half signal only at about **3,000 shots**, roughly 1.5 seasons for a workhorse, and adjusted goals-against rates correlate just 0.296 year to year ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/a-hot-goalie-isnt-a-better-goalie)). Save % above expected correlated at 0.12 in JFresh's sample ([JFresh](https://jfresh.substack.com/p/why-goaltending-is-basically-random?s=r)). Together these imply a beta-binomial prior of roughly **3,000 to more than 11,000 shots**. At those values one standard deviation of true talent is only about 0.003–0.005 of save %, or 0.08–0.16 goals over 30 shots, while single-game binomial noise is about 0.054. Shot volume and the team in front of the goalie therefore matter far more per game than his identity. The regression target must be season-current, because league save % slid from .9149 in 2016 to .9042 in 2023 ([Robnett & Welsh](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/sure/2023/showcase/hockey_saves/report.html)). Shot-quality adjustment helps only slightly ([arXiv 1205.1746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.1746)). Back-to-back fatigue is real but small: raw data show .905 falling to .897 with 0.8 more shots against ([Hockey Graphs](https://hockey-graphs.com/2014/12/01/goaltender-performance-vs-rest/)), so −0.005 to −0.010 of save % is a reasonable prior.

Wins come from team win probability, which de-vigged moneylines supply directly because NHL moneylines settle including overtime and shootouts. About a fifth to a quarter of games are tied after regulation: 27.3% early in 2025-26 ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/47210375/nhl-2025-26-games-shootout-theories-trends-stats-standings)). A three-outcome model (regulation win, tie, regulation loss) therefore beats a binary one for scoring overtime losses. The goalie's win is then roughly P(start) × P(team win | he starts) × P(not pulled). A sportsbook executive values a starting goalie at only **3–5 percentage points of win probability** ([BettorEdge](https://www.bettoredge.com/post/nhl-starting-goalie-betting)). Shots and wins must share one game script, because underdogs face more shots: their saves outlook rises as their win outlook falls.

Shutouts follow Poisson to a first approximation, P(GA = 0) = e^(−λ). At about 3 goals per team-game this gives a 4.8% shutout rate, which tracked an early-season sample of 581 games closely ([Maclean's/338Canada](https://macleans.ca/sports/338canada-now-for-something-lighter-than-politics-nhl-projections)). The probability is steeply sensitive to λ: 8.2% at 2.5 goals against, but 4.1% at 3.2. Because sv^shots is convex in shots, a mixture over the shots-against distribution yields a higher P(SO) than plugging in mean shots, and fantasy shutouts also require the goalie to finish the game.

### Fantasy points are weighted sums of means, except at thresholds

By linearity of expectation, expected fantasy points equal Σ weight × E[stat] exactly, so a points league needs nothing more than unbiased means. FHFH's default Yahoo weights (G 3, A 2, PPP 1, SOG 0.2, HIT 0.2, BLK 0.25; goalie W 4, GA −1, SV 0.2, SHO 3) put most skater value in goals, assists and power-play points ([FORGE audit](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/REPORT.md)). Under those weights a typical starter projects to about 5 FP, using illustrative inputs of P(win) 0.55, 27 saves, 2.8 GA and P(SO) 0.06. Saves supply the largest expected value, while the win term alone swings by about ±2 FP (my arithmetic).

DFS scoring adds threshold bonuses, such as DraftKings' +3 for 5+ shots, 3+ blocks or 35+ saves ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/hockey/article.php?id=47353); [DKNetwork](https://dknetwork.draftkings.com/2020/07/02/beginner-nhl-dfs-scoring/)). These must be valued as bonus × P(X ≥ t). A Poisson(3.5) shooter clears five shots 27.5% of the time, worth 0.82 points, while a rule that pays the bonus only when the projected mean reaches the threshold credits zero and under-projects stars most. Categories leagues need per-category z-scores and volume-weighted ratio stats. DFS sites mostly publish medians with percentile floors and ceilings, but the definitions vary: 85/15 at 4for4, top 15% at FantasyLabs ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/nfl-dfs-floor-ceiling-projections); [Stokastic](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/dfs-strategy/dfs-projections-vs-simulations)). For a right-skewed per-game points distribution the median sits below the mean, so a median looks "low" against realized averages by construction. Points-league tools should headline the mean.

## Markets price distributions, and their lines make strong priors

### Books build props from the same core, then price every threshold

Public prop pricing starts from the same opportunity × rate mean and converts it into a probability at each half-point line. BettorEdge's example turns a 2.6-shot mean into 73.3% over 1.5, 48.2% over 2.5 and 26.4% over 3.5 ([BettorEdge](https://www.bettoredge.com/post/nhl-player-props-explained)). RotoWire's Lamp Lab blends 5v5, PP and PK scoring rates with MoneyPuck xG into a Poisson λ, then adjusts for opponent and opposing goalie at half strength ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/hockey/article/nhl-anytime-goal-scorer-model-137385)). Unabated simulates 10,000 outcomes to price main and alternate lines from one distribution ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/tools/core/props-simulator)). Lines sit near the **median**, not the mean, so treating a right-skewed mean projection as a line makes bettors "bet far too many overs" ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/post/the-biggest-mistake-youre-making-when-betting-nfl-player-props)). Margins are higher on props: about 4–6% on major props, 6–10% on secondary and 10–20% on exotic ([Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/article/player-props-understanding-the-math-behind-the-lines/)). Proportional de-vigging overstates longshots: Pinnacle's longest soccer odds implied 3.9% against 2.6% observed ([Data Golf](https://datagolf.com/fav-longshot-not-a-bias)). Shin-style or equal-margin methods are better for "Yes"-only anytime-goal prices ([penaltyblog](https://penaltyblog.readthedocs.io/en/latest/implied/index.html)).

### NHL prop efficiency is unmeasured, so treat lines as evidence

No peer-reviewed study tests NHL player-prop efficiency; this is a literature gap, not a search failure. Practitioners describe star and high-volume props as efficient, and secondary players and exotic stats as softer but more heavily margined ([Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/article/player-props-understanding-the-math-behind-the-lines/)). The only NHL market evidence concerns game totals. An older study found a 54.2% under win rate over more than 5,000 games ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227410468_Market_Efficiency_and_the_NHL_totals_betting_market_Is_there_an_under_bias)), and a 2018 dissertation found the totals market largely efficient ([UNCO](https://digscholarship.unco.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1526&context=dissertations)).

Information timing is well described. Overnight lines price the projected starter, the morning skate usually confirms him, and saves props are posted only after confirmation. Winnipeg's surprise start for Comrie moved Tampa from pick'em to −145 ([BettorEdge](https://www.bettoredge.com/post/nhl-starting-goalie-betting); [ScoresAndStats](https://www.scoresandstats.com/?p=17721)). Grading rules define the target: overtime counts and the shootout does not, skater props void if the player does not dress, and goalie props void if he does not start ([BettorEdge](https://www.bettoredge.com/post/nhl-player-props-explained)).

### Use lines as implied means and team totals, compared in probability space

Market prices become projection inputs through inversion. For any 0.5 line, the de-vigged over probability gives the mean directly, λ = −ln(1 − p): a 30% anytime price implies 0.357 goals. Higher lines need bisection under Poisson, or a dispersion assumption or an alternate-line ladder under the negative binomial. The highest-value use is team-level. Daily Faceoff derives implied team totals from the game line and total, and builds goalie implied win %, goals against and expected saves from "a combination of team underlying metrics and sportsbook odds" ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/dfo-dfs-charts-04-17-25)). A model can then scale each skater's goal λ, and his on-ice goals for assists, by the ratio of market to model team goals, either fully or with a fitted partial weight. It can damp the same scaling for shots, and check that the sum of implied anytime-goal λs on a team roughly matches its implied total. The market should get more weight where it is sharp (star shots, game totals, confirmed goalies) and less where it is thin or "Yes"-only. The weight should be fitted by minimizing log loss or Brier score on history. No public study shows that market anchoring improves NHL fantasy accuracy, so it has to be backtested, but it is the cheapest available way to absorb late lineup news and league-environment shifts.

### Fantasy providers publish structure, never accuracy

Fantasy and DFS providers converge on the same structure: opportunity (TOI, line, PP unit) × efficiency (shots/60, points/60) × matchup and goalie adjustments. Daily Faceoff projects TOI from current lines ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/dfo-dfs-charts-04-17-25)), Left Wing Lock updates lines and PP units daily and rest-of-season projections weekly ([Left Wing Lock](https://leftwinglock.com/draftkits/index.php?s=faq)), and Dobber starts from Evolving-Hockey ice time ([DobberHockey](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=90455)). None publishes a reproducible methodology or an audited NHL accuracy record. In other sports, consensus projections beat single sources: the best football source explained 50–67% of season-level variance ([Fantasy Football Analytics](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2014/06/which-site-has-the-best-fantasy-football-projections-2014-update/)), and simple Marcel usually ranked second among baseball systems ([RotoValue](https://blog.rotovalue.com/?p=534)). For FORGE, the only defensible benchmarks are naive baselines and the market.

## Score per-game counts with bias, RMSE and proper scores, never MAE alone

The metric must match the quantity forecast. Absolute error is minimized by the median and squared error by the mean ([scoringutils](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/scoringutils/vignettes/scoring-rules.html)). Kolassa shows that MAD, MASE and wMAPE are "inherently unsuitable for count data" ([Kolassa](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v32y2016i3p788-803.html)), and on zero-heavy series MAE "may select models that predict constant 0's" ([Hewamalage et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10716)). For a Poisson with λ below ln 2 ≈ 0.69, which covers nearly every skater's goals per game, MAE rises as the forecast moves from zero toward the true mean. At λ = 0.30, forecasting zero scores MAE 0.300 and MSE 0.390, while forecasting the correct 0.30 scores MAE 0.445 and MSE 0.300. **A correct fix that removes low bias will raise MAE while improving RMSE and calibration.** FORGE's own backtests reproduce this: the even-strength assist refit improved assist RMSE by 0.032 while MAE rose 0.030 ([ES refit verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/assists-es-refit-verdict.md)), and the history fix cut fantasy-point RMSE by 0.089 with MAE flat ([hydration verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/hydration-recon-verdict.md)).

Distributions need proper scoring rules, whose expected score is optimized only by the true predictive distribution ([Gneiting & Raftery](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA459827.pdf)). For counts that means the **log score** and the **ranked probability score**, the discrete analogue of CRPS; the **Dawid–Sebastiani score** works when only a mean and SD are served. Binary events such as a goal, a start or a shutout take the **Brier score** with its reliability/resolution decomposition ([arXiv 2106.14345](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14345)), and randomized or nonrandomized **PIT histograms** should come out flat for a calibrated count model ([Czado, Gneiting & Held](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1541-0420.2009.01191.x)).

Apparent star under-projection has distinct causes that need different fixes. Some are definitional, such as serving a median as the headline or tuning on MAE. Some are evaluation artefacts: binning by outcome instead of by forecast guarantees actual > predicted among players who had big games ([Minitab](https://blog.minitab.com/en/blog/fun-with-statistics/fantasy-studs-and-regression-to-the-mean)). The rest are genuine model biases, namely over-shrinkage toward one mean, opportunity regressed toward average, and bonuses evaluated at the mean. Three diagnostics separate them. The first is Σactual/Σpredicted by forecast-tier decile and position. The second is a Mincer–Zarnowitz regression of outcome on forecast with HAC errors, where a slope above 1 signals over-shrinkage ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/mincer-zarnowitz-forecast-test/)). The third splits error into opportunity (TOI, PP TOI) and rate (per-60), mirroring FantasyPros' separate grading of playing time ([FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2023/01/most-accurate-fantasy-baseball-projections-2022/amp/)).

Backtests must run on a rolling origin with strictly point-in-time inputs, because in-sample residuals understate true forecast error ([Hyndman & Athanasopoulos](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)). The NHL-specific leakage hazards are final box-score TOI used as an input, season-to-date rates that include the target game, goalie confirmations or line combinations captured after lock, ratings recomputed with full-season data, and hyperparameters tuned on the evaluation season.

| Question | Metric | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Is the mean unbiased overall and for stars? | Mean error; Σactual/Σpredicted by forecast decile × position × head | Bin by forecast, never by outcome |
| Is it better than doing nothing clever? | RMSE skill = 1 − loss/loss_baseline vs season-to-date, last-10, prior rate × last-10 TOI, and de-vigged market | Paired on identical player-games, bootstrap CI clustered by game |
| Over- or under-reaction? | Mincer–Zarnowitz slope and intercept | HAC errors; low power in small samples |
| Opportunity or efficiency? | Separate bias tables for TOI/PP TOI and per-60 rates | Fix deployment before rates if TOI explains the tier bias |
| Are the distributions honest? | RPS or log score, PIT histogram, 80% interval coverage | Required once quantiles or P(over line) are served |
| Are probabilities calibrated? | Brier with reliability diagram for goal, start, win and shutout | Bins near p = 0.3 need about 400 player-games for ±2.3 pp |

## FORGE has the right skeleton but stops short of calibration

### Where FORGE already meets the standard

FORGE's architecture is the consensus one: rolling last-5, season and three-year rates shrunk toward priors, team-level TOI and PP-share allocation, shots × conversion for goals, teammate coupling (which Magnus ranks above competition effects), schedule adjustments, goalie start mixtures, and expected fantasy points as weighted sums. Since R19 the PP allocation uses official team usage. That change took team PP targets from **28.5% too high to 5.6% too high** against actuals and removed most of the excess PP minutes the top decile had been given ([PP-usage verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/pp-usage-verdict.md)). FORGE's evaluation is ahead of every public provider surveyed. Its backtest harness scores bias, RMSE and top-decile Σactual/Σpredicted with paired bootstraps clustered by game. Releases are gated on bias, RMSE and calibration rather than MAE. Since R19 a served-forecast ledger also records what users were actually shown, beside naive last-5 and season controls ([served ledger](../../../../web/lib/player-forecasts/servedForecastLedger.ts)). That is precisely the discipline the forecasting literature prescribes.

### Five structural shortfalls remain

**First, assists are tied to the player's own goals.** On master, even-strength assists are computed as the player's projected goals × an assists-per-goal rate clamped between 0.4 and 3.4, and PP assists stay on the old per-two-goals scale ([skater stage](../../../../web/lib/projections/stages/skater-stage.ts); [skater adjustments](../../../../web/lib/projections/calculators/skater-adjustments.ts)). This contradicts the on-ice construction above. A pass-first player's assists depend on his shooting, any shrinkage on conversion leaks into assists, and an elite playmaking defenceman whose assists-to-goals ratio exceeds 3.4 is capped (my inference from the code). FORGE's own backtest has already hit this wall. Moving PP assists to a per-goal scale fixed overall PPP bias but over-predicted top-decile PPP by 29%, which the verdict attributes to "the player's own goals × an assists-per-goal rate… which over-credits high-volume goal scorers with assists" ([PP-usage verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/pp-usage-verdict.md)). Actual PP assists run about **1.8 per PP goal** against a modelled ~0.95 ([PP diagnosis](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/pp-overprediction-diagnosis.md)). The "halved assists" fix is therefore only half shipped, and the remaining half is blocked by model structure rather than tuning.

**Second, priors are global constants and the league environment is frozen.** Even-strength and PP shot rates are blended toward fixed 6 and 8 shots/60 with a window-blind sample weight of about 0.7 for veterans. Conversion is then shrunk a second time toward fixed 0.095 and 0.145 goals per shot, after an adaptive Bayes prior has already shrunk it ([FORGE audit](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/REPORT.md)). Those are single league means for every forward and defenceman, the over-shrinkage pattern FanGraphs and Magnus avoid, and none is re-estimated per season despite the documented shifts in shot volume and goals per shot. FORGE also has no aging term or rookie discount in the skater stage. Removing the double shrink did not clear an earlier backtest ([Q7/Q8 verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/q7-q8-backtest-verdict.md)). That is consistent with errors offsetting one another, not with the shrink being right, so it should be retested once history hydration lands.

**Third, market data is collected but used backwards.** FORGE stores game and player-prop prices and compares the model **mean** with the books' **average line** ([market builders](../../../../web/lib/projections/utils/market-output-builders.ts)). An "over" or "under" edge flag fires when the gap passes a fixed threshold (0.2 goals, 0.5 shots). This is the mean-versus-median error the betting literature warns about, and it ignores price entirely. For a goals over/under posted at 0.5, every player projected at 0.3 goals or fewer gets an "under" flag whatever the odds (my inference from the threshold). No implied team total, moneyline or de-vigged probability feeds the projections or the evaluation.

**Fourth, goalie save % leans on recent form and wins inherit skater bias.** FORGE first shrinks season save % toward the goalie's career with a 900-shot prior. It then moves toward the recent window with a 300-shot prior (half weight once that window holds 300 shots), and only then shrinks toward the league with a prior of **350–1,250 shots** ([goalie model](../../../../web/lib/projections/goalieModel.ts)). For a veteran with about 4,900 shots of evidence the final league weight is about 7% (my arithmetic), against 38–69% under the 3,000–11,000-shot range the reliability studies imply. That is exactly the "hot goalie" weighting Paine and JFresh warn against. Win probability is a logistic of skater-summed goals for minus goals against (scale 1.25) × P(start). It ignores moneylines, overtime structure and pull risk, and inherits any skater under-projection through goals for. The interim bridge meanwhile uses a different, Poisson-xG win model with ties split ([bridge](../../../../web/lib/rosterScheduleData/forgeDailyBridge.ts)). Shutouts use e^(−GA) × P(start), a sound first approximation without the shots mixture.

**Fifth, uncertainty and evaluation stop at Poisson and point metrics.** Quantiles come from Poisson draws that the code itself labels "MVP uncertainty scaffolding" ([uncertainty](../../../../web/lib/projections/uncertainty.ts)), with no negative binomial dispersion for shots, hits, blocks or saves and no team-level correlation. The accuracy route reports MAE, RMSE, interval coverage and Brier calibration. Neither it nor the ledger scoreboard reports RPS, log score, PIT, Mincer–Zarnowitz slopes, opportunity-versus-rate splits or a market benchmark, and the scoreboard compares sources as unpaired samples ([scoreboard](../../../../web/pages/api/v1/forge/scoreboard.ts)). The reconstruction backtests read today's database, not point-in-time inputs. The verdict itself flags this, and the ledger fixes it only going forward ([hydration verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/hydration-recon-verdict.md)).

Context and physical stats add smaller gaps. FORGE's home-ice term works out to roughly +2% at home and −1% away on goals, against roughly ±4% in the evidence (my arithmetic from the multipliers). Rest multiplies four heads at once. The proposed back-to-back fix (Q8) would cut a tired team's goals by about 17% through compounding, far beyond the 3–4% the evidence supports. The backtest already scored it a wash, so it should not ship as written. Hits and blocks have no rink or scorekeeper term. Once hydration supplies full history, hits flip from **−0.23 to +0.26** bias and the top-decile ratio falls to 0.636 ([hydration verdict](../../forge-projection-audit-2026-10-21/hydration-recon-verdict.md)). That is why the F/D-prior hits/blocks rework must ship alongside hydration.

## Conclusion

FORGE's under-projection was a stack of offsetting errors rather than one bias. Over-allocated power-play minutes were hidden by halved assists and missing history, and each fix exposed the next: the PP fix moved top-decile PPP from about 4% to 12% under-projected, and hydration flipped hits from under to over. The lesson for gating is that a single fantasy-point bias number can look healthy while two heads are wrong in opposite directions. The durable instrument is the per-head, forecast-binned, opportunity-versus-rate panel run on paired served forecasts, which FORGE is one step from having. The research also explains why the remaining assist gap resisted rescaling: tying assists to the player's own goals is a structural departure from how every rigorous model treats point production, not a parameter error.

The largest unclaimed lever is external anchoring. Public projections overshot by 6–11% when power-play opportunities and shot volume fell; FORGE hard-codes its league priors, yet already stores the sportsbook prices that would re-anchor it nightly. And because per-game outcomes are dominated by noise (a 0.35-goal scorer fails to score in 70% of games, and goalie talent spread is a tenth of single-game noise), the next accuracy gains will come from honest distributions, calibrated probabilities and market-consistent team totals rather than from more talent features. Public research offers no NHL stabilization points, dispersion values, prop-efficiency evidence or per-game accuracy benchmarks. FORGE's served-forecast ledger is therefore likely to become the best evidence available for those questions, provided it records market prices and point-in-time inputs alongside its own numbers.

# FORGE v2: parallel skater forecast plan

Status: planning only, 2026-10-29. This document does not authorize implementation, production capture, database changes, a deployment, or a change to FORGE v1 or RSO. The companion [task list](tasks-prd-forge-v2-skater-forecasts.md) records proposed work and dependencies.

## Product contract

FORGE v2 produces **individual skater forecasts for the next 10 scheduled games of the player's current team**, one separately contextualized record per player and target game. There is one horizon: H1–H10 are team-game ordinals, not calendar days or a second sequence of predicted player appearances. No team-total or final-score forecasts are requested. Goalie design is a separate future project.

For each identified skater, show the target game, opponent, current ordinal, forecast fantasy points and scoring profile, issue time, data freshness, and explicit participation/dressing uncertainty. A player who might not dress still has a target-game record; a scratch does not move that game to a different identity. If a team, schedule, roster, inputs, or participation estimate cannot be established, show a reasoned unavailable or stale state, not an invented value. At season end or with incomplete schedule evidence, show the supported games and disclose why fewer than 10 are available.

Working estimand for the v2 point and interval: fantasy points **conditional on the skater dressing**, using a versioned default-league scoring profile. Show the separately estimated probability of dressing when supported. An unconditional expected value may be derived as probability × conditional mean only when both are valid; unknown participation is not zero or 100%. Explicitly label conditioning so no-shows are not confused with low conditional forecasts. Preserve any existing league-specific RSO scoring on its own track; this plan does not authorize changing it.

## Identity, issuance and final pregame selection

1. Resolve a skater's team and a stable official `game_id` from evidence available at the issue cutoff. Order eligible future games by scheduled start and stable game ID; assign H1–H10 at that issuance. Horizon is metadata, not identity: when game 10 becomes game 9, the same player/game chain continues.
2. Atomically commit each successful issued version with immutable player/game/team identity; ordinal; source-availability cutoff and issue/commit time; schedule revision and start known then; trigger; conditional point and any participation/interval outputs; target, scoring, code, model and calibration versions/hashes; provenance and quality/fallback flags. A retry of the **same issuance** is idempotent; a genuinely reissued value, including an unchanged numerical value, gets a distinct version. Failed or partial work is observable and must not masquerade as an issued forecast. Preserve every issued version, including versions later found ineligible.
3. Read all versions in stable `(issued_at, issuance_id)` order without a silent row cap. Preserve as-issued start and schedule evidence; distinguish a postponed, rescheduled or cancelled game and a trade/roster change. A change in ordinal or team must not rewrite old versions or attach them to a different game. Incompatible target, scoring or conditioning definitions form separate series; comparable model-version changes remain visible and are marked.
4. The final eligible v2 pregame projection is the latest successfully committed, complete, appropriately versioned forecast whose inputs were available by its cutoff and whose issue time is **strictly before the game's authoritative actual start**. Before actual start is known, use the trustworthy scheduled start/status known at issuance conservatively and mark the result provisional; unknown or stale start/status cannot silently pass the gate. Reconcile when authoritative timing arrives; deterministic tie-breaking, timing corrections and post-start issues are auditable without modifying issued records. A cancelled game is void; an unplayed or unsettled game is pending until its status is known. Missing pregame coverage stays missing.
5. Settle the final eligible projection against the same skater/game's authoritative outcome and scoring profile. Append provisional, final and corrected outcome/evaluation revisions; never overwrite forecast history. For conditional FP error and interval coverage, grade dressed skaters with verified outcomes; count non-dressers separately and evaluate their participation probabilities over the full eligible population.

## Refresh behavior and cost control

Refresh after each skater appearance and, when a team game starts or completes, update that team's eligible schedule for all affected skaters even if an individual did not dress; add the new H10 when the game leaves the future schedule and refresh again as qualifying results arrive. Trigger another refresh when accepted, materially different source evidence affects the forecast: injury/availability or scratch status, recall/assignment/trade and roster identity, meaningful lineup/role or opponent context, schedule/start/status changes, relevant finalized or corrected statistics, or an explicitly released model/calibration/scoring version. A materiality policy and freshness/deadline targets must be frozen with the implementation, including which downstream players and games each change affects. Do not pretend to refresh on news the existing ingestion never observed.

Use existing ingested evidence and its watermarks, not ten new external fetches per player. Queue bounded work by affected team/player/game, reuse shared inputs, deduplicate repeated source events, coalesce events before issuance when deadlines permit, and recompute only affected scopes. Coalescing never deletes an already issued version. A source update arriving during a run must leave a follow-up pending; stale, failed, missing or delayed refreshes are visible and retryable. If required inputs are late, retain the last valid vintage with a stale reason and issue a new version when qualifying evidence arrives; do not manufacture a timely refresh.

Before any real scheduler or capture is enabled, measure local/fixture CPU time, row/byte volume, queue depth and worst-case fan-out. Budget initial work approximately as eligible skaters × up to 10 game records; budget subsequent work by affected player-games × actual material refreshes, plus indexes and retained outcome versions. Set explicit concurrency, batch, runtime, storage and request limits from measured results; reuse shared caches and admission controls. The repository's NST rolling limits still apply to all participating jobs, and new provider acquisition or hosted schedules require separate bounded authorization. This plan does not assert a cost or activate a cron.

## Two different kinds of uncertainty

**Forecast overlap.** For one player/game and comparable target, join successive eligible pregame point forecasts in time order; preserve and separately label any post-start or otherwise ineligible issuance instead of letting it distort the pregame path. A segment paints its entire low-to-high span at 25% opacity; overlapping segments compound opacity as `1 - (1 - 0.25)^n`, where `n` is the number of traversals. Display the observed high/low, issuance count and, if useful, the unweighted mean of issued values. A single value is a marker; an unchanged adjacent value adds no range. The color shows revision-path overlap, not a probability, confidence interval, time-weighted density or guarantee of an outcome; it depends on issuance cadence. Preserve all versions behind the visualization and provide an accessible textual/table equivalent.

For Larkin's `10 → 5 → 8 → 6.5`, the high is 10, low is 5, and mean is 7.375. The ranges 8–10, 5–6.5 and 6.5–8 have one, two and three traversals respectively, giving 25%, 43.75% and about 57.8% compounded opacity (endpoints have no area). H10 → H9 → H8 → H7 refers to the same game ID.

**Statistical interval — core scope.** Provide a calibrated, clearly labeled prediction interval for the actual single-game fantasy-point outcome, conditional on dressing, separately from the overlap graphic. This is the practical interpretation of the requested statistical uncertainty range: a prediction interval concerns a future game; a confidence interval for the mean is a different quantity and must not be mislabeled. Start with a versioned nominal 80% interval (p10–p90) for the agreed scoring profile, with room for other levels only after the same evaluation. Derive it from a joint fantasy-point distribution or directly calibrated FP errors; do not add independent per-stat quantiles or imply known correlations.

Build or calibrate a dedicated uncertainty model if the native FORGE distribution is not empirically adequate. Freeze the model, calibration method, training cutoff, temporal splits, cohort, nominal coverage, minimum evidence and pass tolerances **before** inspecting the final evaluation. Use rolling-origin/as-of data, with no future news or outcomes; separate model fitting, calibration, untouched final evaluation and later prospective issued evidence. Verify input vintages exist rather than label reconstructions as original live forecasts. Report empirical coverage with slate/time-block uncertainty, interval width and a proper interval/distribution score, overall and by H1–H10 where supported; report sample sizes, participation conditioning, missing forecasts and abstentions. Compare against matched baselines on the same player/game/cutoff/scoring cohort. Evaluate dressing probabilities separately with reliability and Brier/log loss. Insufficient data, unsupported slices, failed calibration or unknown drift means an explicit unavailable/not-yet-calibrated interval and a blocked v2 readiness gate, not a cosmetic band or a deferred post-launch task.

## Accountability and verification

Maintain two clearly labeled, non-substitutable views:

1. **RSO served/displayed v1:** preserve the approved game-day grade, default scoring, paired last-five baseline, dressed-only error and separate no-show count. Establish whether the existing bridge snapshot actually matches a displayed value; show its evidence class and any missing/mismatched exposure. Do not silently replace it with a later v2 forecast or treat its historical checkpoints as every issued revision.
2. **FORGE v2:** use the final eligible pregame version above for overall MAE, RMSE, signed bias, sample/forecast coverage and paired last-five comparison; show interval coverage/width/proper score and participation calibration separately. Provide optional H1–H10 / elapsed-time comparisons on matched cohorts and a player/game drill-down with all issued values, high/low, overlap, interval and actual. Four old calendar checkpoints may be a summary, never the full revision history. Distinguish retrospective experiments, prospective shadow, and any later actual serving; no history is invented before capture.

End-to-end acceptance must use deterministic local fixtures first: ten distinct player/game outputs; the same target moves H10→H9→H8→H7 after completed games and retains the `10 → 5 → 8 → 6.5` chain; extra material reissues and idempotent retries behave correctly; all history survives pagination and model changes; schedule corrections/trade/cancellation, no-show, missing evidence, late/stale data and post-start rejection are explicit; final selection, revised actuals, paired baseline, opacity and calibrated-interval reporting are correct. Confirm v1 and RSO fixture outputs remain unchanged and run focused model, persistence, API and accessible UI tests. Prove the statistical gate on a preregistered untouched temporal evaluation and, when separately authorized, prospective shadow issuance/settlement with bounded resources. Existing code, local fixtures, or an earlier release alone cannot substitute for that evidence.

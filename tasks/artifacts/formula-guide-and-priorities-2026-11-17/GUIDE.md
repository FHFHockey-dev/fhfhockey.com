# What the FHFH calculations mean

November 17, 2026 · Companion to the [searchable calculation index](CALCULATIONS.html) and the [project assessment](PROJECTS.md).

A manager asking “Should I start this skater?” needs three different numbers. How much does he produce when he plays? How likely is he to play? Can he actually fit into a scoring slot? A defensible rate can still give the wrong lineup advice if the next step supplies the wrong minutes, applies the probability twice, or counts a bench game.

The November 10 inventory has 1,358 records in 23 families. Records include alternate versions, models, SQL views, helper functions and possible calculations identified by symbol. The November 14 review used a different unit, recording 1,078 **paths**, including 26 with no math. Its 417 findings belong to that review's fixed version. Neither number is a count of distinct user-facing scores or currently broken calculations. The index covers the 1,358 inventory IDs individually. The guide groups repeated arithmetic and uses the later decisions where the difference changes interpretation. ## Reading the numbers

- **Per game** needs a named game population. Four goals in eight appearances is 0.50 per appearance. If the team played ten games, the same four goals are 0.40 per team game.
- **Per 60 minutes** = 60 × total events ÷ minutes of matching ice time. Three shots in 15 minutes is 12 shots per 60. If the stored clock is seconds, the multiplier is 3,600. A 5v5 rate needs a 5v5 numerator and clock.
- **A pooled percentage** = 100 × total successes ÷ total opportunities. A goalie who saves 9 of 10 and 20 of 25 saves 29 of 35, or 82.86%. Averaging 90% and 80% gives 85% and gives the short appearance the same influence as the long one.
- **Percentage points** measure subtraction. Moving from 8% to 10% is +2 percentage points and a +25% relative change. A proportion stored as 0.10 is displayed as 10%.
- **Missing, zero and ineligible** lead to different decisions. A confirmed absence can contribute zero to an unconditional game forecast. An absent projection leaves the value unknown. A zero opportunity denominator makes a rate undefined. Historical entries in the index include implementations that violated these distinctions.

## From events to team and player descriptions

| Calculation family | Formula or operating rule | Hockey interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Expected goals, xG | For a logistic model, shot probability p = 1 ÷ (1 + exp(−s)), where s is the intercept plus the sum of each encoded feature × its fitted coefficient. Ordinary xG = sum of p across eligible shots. | A 0.2 shot and a 0.3 shot contribute 0.5 expected goals. Shot eligibility, features, model version and calibration matter. The audit found leakage in an older champion. The later one-bend challenger improved overall loss but missed protected subgroup criteria. TJ's recorded decision retained the incumbent for that comparison. |
| NST-derived stats | Attempts = goals + saved shots + misses + blocks against the shooter. Unblocked attempts omit blocks. Share for = 100 × events for ÷ (for + against). On-ice PDO on the percentage scale = on-ice shooting % + on-ice save %. | A team with 8% shooting and 92% saves has PDO 100. Provider filters, score state and strength define which events count. The legacy distance-based danger rule is a different measure from a fitted probability or an NST-sourced value. |
| Underlying summaries | SV% = 100 × saves ÷ shots against. GAA = 60 × goals against ÷ goalie minutes. GSAx = expected goals against − actual goals against. GSAA = shots against × (1 − league save fraction) − goals against. | Positive GSAx or GSAA means fewer goals conceded than the specified comparison expected. The comparisons use different information. On 100 shots with a .900 league save fraction and eight actual goals against, GSAA is +2. |
| Rolling player averages and trends | Mean of the last k eligible observations = sum ÷ k. Sample variance = sum of squared deviations from the mean ÷ (n−1). A recency-weighted mean = sum(weight × value) ÷ sum(weights). | The last five recorded values and the last five team games can be different dates. The full-history trend helper skips missing values and waits for k valid values. Other readers use appearance or calendar windows. |
| Lines, TOI and PP units | Player PP share = 100 × seconds the player was on the ice during qualified team power plays ÷ team PP clock seconds. A line builder can instead group players by shared shift overlap and lineup evidence. | Two player-minutes in four team PP minutes is 50%. Summing five skaters' PP seconds would count the same clock time several times. A unit assignment says who was grouped together and requires separate evidence. The Nov 13 closeout records the clock-backed share release. |
| Player cards and homepage | Recent change = recent reading − comparison reading. Card xG share = 100 × total xGF ÷ (total xGF + total xGA). For an equal-window comparison take the N newest appearances and the previous N. | A five-appearance comparison needs ten appearances. A season-to-date baseline can include the recent games. Compare totals only when exposures are compatible. Other homepage scores and artwork helpers have their own entries. |
| WiGO | For its midrank percentile, 100 × (number worse + ½ × number tied) ÷ cohort size. Its Game Score is a weighted sum of personal production, penalties, faceoffs and on-ice results, with five- and ten-game means. | A percentile answers where a reading stands in a specified group. A difference between two time windows describes an association. It cannot by itself tell how the player would have performed with another linemate. Exact Game Score weights are in M-WIGO-003. |
| Rankings | In the contextual ranking helper, percentile = 100 × strictly worse qualified peers ÷ (N−1) for N > 1. Reverse the ordering for lower-is-better metrics. Ties share dense rank. That helper assigns a lone qualifier 100. | Ties and cohort choice explain why Rankings and WiGO can give different percentiles for identical observations. A published Rankings/composite pair also needs a common cutoff and complete cohort. |
| Team ratings, CTPI and strength of schedule | In documented CTPI v2, combine offense .35, defense .30, goaltending .20 and special teams .15. Renormalize over available component weights, subject to coverage. Score = 100 ÷ (1 + exp(−0.6 × raw)). At least five recent games and .65 component-weight coverage are required in the inventory version. | A raw score of zero maps to 50. This describes recent team form. SOS asks how difficult the scheduled opponents are. Neither supplies a qualified game win probability. The debrief distinguishes the live descriptive beta from future predictive checkpoints. |
| Stats helpers | Standard score z = (value − peer mean) ÷ peer standard deviation. Shrunk mean = weight × observed rate + (1−weight) × reference rate. Examples also include decay weights and percentile lookups. | A z of +1 means one standard deviation above that reference group. Small samples can be pulled toward an appropriate reference. The calling metric determines the units, population and zero-spread handling. |

## From observations to expectations

| Calculation family | Formula or operating rule | What it tells a manager |
|---|---|---|
| FORGE game projections | For a rate-based count, expected events if playing = events per 60 × expected minutes ÷ 60. With zero events when absent, unconditional expectation = P(plays) × if-playing expectation. Add strength-specific contributions on matching bases. Model stages also allocate team opportunity, shots and assists. | A skater expected to shoot 12 times per 60 over 15 minutes projects for three shots if dressed. At 80% participation the pregame mean is 2.4. Multiplying a stored 2.4 by 80% again understates it. A missing reserve line cannot supply that estimate. |
| Player forecasts, season/rest of season | Remaining expected total = sum of unconditional future game means. A documented resolver blends compatible detailed and baseline means as w × detailed + (1−w) × baseline. Apply participation once. For a season total including observed play, add actuals through the cutoff. | Means add even when games are correlated. Low/high season outcomes require a joint uncertainty model. Adding each game's upper bound does not produce the season's upper bound. Detailed and season models can use different versions and eligibility rules. |
| Goalie starters and starter odds | Recent start share = credited starts ÷ eligible team games in the window. A starting-goalie forecast can use a model instead. If relief and absence are possible, expected saves = P(start) × mean saves if starting + P(relief) × mean saves in relief. | Six starts in ten games describes usage. Calling it a next-game probability requires pregame-only inputs and evaluation. The opening goalie can differ from the goalie who wins or logs the most time. An expected-if-starts line and an unconditional line answer different questions. |
| Game predictions and odds | For binary outcomes y = 0 or 1, Brier score = average of (p−y)². Log loss averages −ln(p) for an actual win and −ln(1−p) for a loss. For American odds +a, implied probability = 100 ÷ (a+100). For −a, it is a ÷ (a+100), with a positive in both expressions. | A model giving the winner 70% incurs Brier loss 0.09. Book prices include margin. A private capture proves the inputs and output saved for that game. Predictive skill needs fixed-time, representative pregame forecasts evaluated against later results. |
| Sustainability | Example comparison = (current metric − baseline mean) ÷ baseline spread. A documented historical luck-pressure score negates a weighted sum of shooting, on-ice shooting, individual points participation and PP shooting z-scores. Baseline weights, clocks and scales vary across generations. | A player can score less because his shot rate fell, his minutes fell, or fewer shots went in. A historical deviation measures unusualness. The probability that it persists is a different quantity. |
| sKO | The Sko Element target gap D = expected − observed per team game. With league weights, D = sum over categories of weight × (expected − observed), conditional on fresh, compatible evidence. A separate legacy SQL score uses tanh of a weighted sum of standardized metrics. | Positive D is rebound evidence on that measure. TJ's intended product asks whether the player's level is supported versus his established baseline, accounting for opportunity. Several systems share the name sKO. Each has a different interpretation. |
| VuDu | For the documented on-net adapter, save gap in percentage points = 100 × (expected save probability − total saves ÷ total shots on net). Observed GAA and start share describe results and workload separately. | An expected .915 versus an observed .905 gives +1.0 percentage point. TJ's broader goalie sustainability question also needs shot context, workload, relief and availability. The inventoried expected-ratio adapter could return pending. |
| Buy Low / Sell High | The documented evidence index starts with expected production = baseline rates × observed exposure. Subtract observed production, scale the three track gaps for their uncertainty and correlation into d, then index = 100d ÷ (1 + abs(d)). M-BLSH-008 gives the covariance and weights. | Positive means production fell short of the exposure-adjusted baseline. A value of +50 is an index reading. Trade price, probability of a rebound, and whether acquiring the player improves this roster each require additional evidence. |

## From expectations to fantasy decisions

| Calculation family | Formula or operating rule | Manager interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Fantasy scoring | FP = sum of league weight × stat. The Starter Board skater default in M-FSC-001 uses 3G + 2A + PPP + .2 SOG + .2 HIT + .25 BLK. There are other named profiles. | One goal, one PP point and four shots gives 4.8 under that default. A manager's custom weights can change the answer. Category leagues track category effects and pooled goalie ratios. Week Score in this family measures schedule opportunity and has its own version and withholding rules. |
| RSO roster optimizer | Choose the legal player-to-slot assignments with the highest value under the selected objective. Most Starts counts active games. Projected Points sums eligible assigned point values. Plan gain = candidate total − baseline total. DUST rate = bench games ÷ scheduled games in the documented evaluator. | An extra scheduled game helps only if the player fits a scoring slot on that date. A lock, eligibility rule, goalie minimum or acquisition limit can change the best plan. Category comparisons can produce trade-offs with no single winner. |
| Trade value | For comparable forecasts, points gain = sum of incoming league-weighted expectations − sum of outgoing ones. Category gain is calculated per category, pooling the components of goalie ratios. Capacity checks count the additional roster drops required. | Gaining six projected points could still cost a starting slot or force another drop. An observed trade records what happened in that league. Acceptance odds, pick valuation and a single fair price are separate claims. |
| Draft tools | In the documented mock-draft ADP, first average each contributor's eligible picks of the player, then average those contributor means. At least five qualifying contributors are needed in that version. ADP movement = previous pick − current pick. | Moving from pick 50 to pick 40 is a rise of ten places. Averaging people first prevents one prolific mock drafter from dominating. Provider ADP, value above replacement and the best available player for this particular roster are different measures. |
| Miscellaneous | Methods range from points percentage = standings points ÷ (2 × games played) to identity matching, choosing eligible observations, allocating chart coordinates and recording a dated snapshot. | A matching or admission rule answers “which record can we trust here?” Some records simply pass through another calculation or withhold it. For these, the index gives the documented purpose, evidence or unresolved detail. |

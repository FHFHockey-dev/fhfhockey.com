### Stop semantics and outstanding reservations

A database stop serializes against new admissions and blocks new reservations. It **cannot recall an already reserved request**: the worker may already hold a reservation and may send or finish that one provider call after the DB stop commits. The route checks its in-process runtime gate again before dispatch, but that is not another database stop read. Environment/deployment changes are also not an immediate global cancellation guarantee.

Treat every outstanding reservation as potentially spent and keep the full reservation against the aggregate cap. On timeout, lost response or stop with an outstanding reservation, reconcile the existing job, attempt and provider/request receipt without resending or creating another job. Abort is not evidence that the provider did no work. A stop is therefore “no new admissions,” not “zero more in-flight activity.” The pilot's one-call/full-reservation cap bounds that residual exposure.

## Retention and serialization limits

The job loader reads a private owner-staged copy of a retained receipt and prepared packet. It does not fetch from the live receiver/archive or establish source permission/original-reporter authenticity. The operator must stage it from verified immutable retention, record its provenance reference and compute the existing `identityDigest` values. The source receipt is independently stored and compared with `input.packet.receiptSnapshot`. Browser/webhook callers cannot submit source text, candidate IDs or trust claims to this route.

Hash-bearing input, receipt and result JSON is stored as TEXT and parsed at the application boundary. JSONB key reordering would invalidate the existing order-sensitive digests. Result JSON retains both the original held review and any separately bound coordinate candidate; no candidate is automatically applied. The RPC envelope may use JSONB, but digest-bearing documents remain strings.

One approval policy is intentionally retained with all attempts; there is no reset/resume/enrollment API. A process crash after claim leaves the job running; a lost reservation retains its exposure. Neither is reclaimed automatically. A stop closes later admission but cannot retract a provider request already admitted/sent. Historical uncertain exposure must be included in an explicitly approved future policy, not discarded or inferred settled. This candidate does not touch the old local ledger.

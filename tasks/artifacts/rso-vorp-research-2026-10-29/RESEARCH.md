# Fantasy hockey VORP and RSO drop retention

Research and implementation proposal, 29 October 2026. Audience: TJ, the RSO owner, and Claude. This is a planning document, not a code change, a measured positional premium, or evidence that a model has been validated.

## Decision in plain language

RSO is currently doing what it was designed to do: it seeks useful starts or projected points in the selected planning window. But a drop can improve that window while leaving a weaker roster afterward. A defenseman with fewer projected points can be more valuable to retain than a forward if the manager can replace the forward more easily **and** the defenseman supplies starts the roster would otherwise struggle to fill. Whether that is true depends on this particular league and roster, not on the letter D.

Value over replacement player (VORP; also called value above replacement) asks how much a player contributes compared with a realistically obtainable alternative. For a simple same-position, same-opportunity points comparison:

> projected player contribution − projected replacement contribution = projected value over that replacement

This is a *concept*, not one universal formula. A useful in-season RSO application should compare the **whole proposed transaction** with holding the roster. Preserve the existing selected-window gain exactly as reported, then separately estimate the roster's impact on later, non-overlapping dates. This later comparison must account for the added player, the dropped player, other roster members, legal lineup slots, and plausible replacements. Show the manager both time horizons, a safer alternative when supported, and no move. Any rule for combining or ranking these tradeoffs is a product decision to make after validation.

For intuition only, suppose D is projected for 3.0 points per usable start, and an obtainable D substitute is projected for 1.5; a forward is projected for 4.0, with an obtainable forward substitute at 3.5. On genuinely comparable starts, the gaps are 1.5 and 0.5 points respectively. If each could really provide six such starts, the illustrative gaps are 9 and 3 points. This deliberately simple example is **not** a league estimate and does not handle a real roster's competing players, schedules, or the incoming player. The complete lineup comparison below is the decision model.

## What the sources support

### Hockey evidence

[Daily Faceoff's customizable fantasy hockey rankings](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/how-to-using-the-fantasy-hockey-customizable-rankings-tool) describe using league scoring and platform eligibility to compare projected production with a replacement level at each position. Its explicit example is that a higher-scoring forward is not necessarily more valuable than a defenseman or goalie. This is evidence for the *concept*, not proof of any RSO-specific baseline, D premium, in-season waiver model, or realized improvement.

[NHL Fantasy's schedule guide](https://www.nhl.com/news/topic/fantasy/fantasy-hockey-schedule-cheat-sheet-for-rest-of-season) treats upcoming games and streaming as actionable hockey context. In daily lineup formats, timing and slot congestion matter: an NHL game adds no fantasy start if a player cannot be legally assigned to an active slot, while an off-night game may fit when a busy night cannot. [ESPN's lineup instructions](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000958652-Setting-Your-Lineup) describe daily active/bench decisions and position eligibility. These observations motivate a date- and roster-aware calculation; they do not measure its accuracy.

### Related evidence and what does not transfer automatically

[FantasyPros' football explanation](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/06/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-value-based-drafting-vorp-vols-vona/) uses the expected waiver-wire option as a replacement reference and estimates it from roster depth. The common idea transfers to hockey, but a draft-time positional cutoff is not the same as an in-season league-specific acquisition. Hockey's more frequent games, multi-position eligibility, daily/weekly lineup rules, game congestion, goaltenders and category/ratio scoring require their own treatment. Football can also have flex positions and streaming; the difference is one of league rules and scheduling, not an exclusive hockey feature.

[Rosenof's fantasy basketball paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02188) studies category aggregation and uncertainty under a simplified basketball model and finds improved simulation results for its G-score in that setting. It is not a hockey validation or a reason to deploy G-scores here: the paper's model and simulations have their own assumptions, including simplified position rules. [Elmachtoub and Grigas' predict-then-optimize work](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005) motivates measuring the consequences of decisions, not just forecast error. Its examples are not RSO or hockey, and it does not validate a particular training method here. [Google's assignment example](https://developers.google.com/optimization/assignment/assignment_example) illustrates one-to-one assignment constraints, not an off-the-shelf complete fantasy roster optimizer.

### Provider rules and access limits

Provider and league settings are authoritative for the manager's actual league. [ESPN's waiver documentation](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000036711-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers) distinguishes immediately available free agents from conditional waiver claims. [ESPN's hockey game-limit guidance](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058934911-Games-Played-Limits-Maximums) also demonstrates that some leagues cap games and that the precise cutoff rule is provider-specific. Do not apply those ESPN details to a Yahoo or custom league without verified rules.

The requested Yahoo help pages for [hockey eligibility](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN7058.html) and [transaction and lineup deadlines](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/fantasy-hockey/sln6775.html) returned HTTP 429 on direct access during this research. Their search-index snippets were visible, but their full contents were **not verified**. Recheck the actual league's position eligibility, locks, transaction timing and waiver settings through authorized provider evidence; do not infer detailed Yahoo rules from this report.

## Proposed method: whole-roster counterfactuals

### 1. Use a frozen, evidence-bearing decision snapshot

At decision time `t`, retain the league, team, scoring rules, position/slot eligibility, active/bench/reserve capacity, roster, schedule revisions, locked assignments, acquisition periods and remaining allowances, waiver status/clearance/budget where verified, and forecast provenance/permissions as of `t`. Treat a verified free agent differently from a conditional waiver claim and from a manager-supplied availability assertion. Record freshness and completeness of each input.

Identify obtainable alternatives from the **actual league's** verified available pool at the cutoff. Include players only when identity, provider eligibility, acquisition timing, and rules support the hypothetical; do not use a platform-wide ownership percentage as availability. An eligible alternative may cover the affected slot indirectly through a multi-position teammate; it need not share the dropped player's nominal position. A no-replacement or incomplete-pool scenario is a limitation, not proof that a missing candidate scores zero. If several candidates are plausible, show the attainable options and sensitivity to which one is actually acquired. Recheck future availability at action time; avoid promising a future claim will succeed.

Before implementation, inventory whether the existing RSO snapshot actually has an adequate complete available pool, schedules and *admitted, comparable forecasts* beyond its current selected window. Forecast permission and coverage cannot be assumed from the existence of a future date or another FORGE proposal. If the inputs are missing, report future impact as unknown and preserve the current RSO behavior; do not fabricate a baseline.

### 2. Define two windows and carry the real plan state

Let `W` be the exact scoring opportunities already included in the current RSO selected window, respecting its start and end dates, as-of cutoff, and the provider's game attribution. Let `F` begin immediately after the end of `W` and end at an explicitly chosen later boundary `h`. These are disjoint in league-local time and game attribution. If the selected window is not the current week, label it accurately. The later horizon and any weighting of periods remain product decisions, not settled model parameters; disclose the exact dates and any truncated coverage.

For each legal proposal `P`, evaluate its timed steps through the end of `W`, including an action submitted before the selected start when it becomes effective within the window. Carry the resulting roster, reserve/lock state, used and remaining transaction budgets, and conditional claim branches into `F`. Do the same for the no-move path `N`. A multi-leg streaming plan carries the **last actually held streamer** and all preceding drops/transactions at the boundary; it does not magically restore the original roster. If a claim fails, evaluate the appropriate no-claim continuation rather than charging or crediting success as certain.

For points leagues, with a common, predeclared continuation policy `π` and comparable admitted inputs:

```
ΔW(P) = current RSO selected-window value(P) − current RSO selected-window value(N)
ΔF(P | π) = future roster value(state after P, F | π)
             − future roster value(state after N, F | π)
```

`ΔW` is the existing result, not a VORP-adjusted number. `ΔF` is a **whole-roster** difference and can be positive or negative: it includes the incoming player, the departing player, displaced starts, position flexibility and any legal later actions assumed by `π`. In additive points scoring, `ΔW + ΔF` can be displayed as a modelled total only if both periods use compatible units, complete coverage, non-overlapping dates and the same stated assumptions. Do not silently choose a cross-period weight, discount rate or ranking policy. H2H period outcomes, playoff value and category utilities need their own manager-approved objective rather than an automatic sum of fantasy points.

An illustrative output might be: drop D/add A, `ΔW = +5`, `ΔF = −9`; drop F/add A, `ΔW = +3`, `ΔF = −2`; no move, `0` and `0`. These are invented whole-roster results, not empirical estimates. They illustrate why a current-window winner can have a worse later consequence. A genuinely strong incoming player can equally make `ΔF` positive even when the player dropped was valuable.

### 3. Make continuation assumptions symmetric and modest

The first testable baseline can hold each boundary roster fixed through `F`, allowing legal lineup assignments but **no additional transactions**. This yields a transparent conditional scenario, not a claim that a manager will really hold the final streamer. It includes the initial plan's transaction cost/state, but does not model the value of future moves unless the chosen utility accounts for that resource. Report that omission.

This fixed-roster comparison captures scarcity **inside the resulting roster** and the effect of the actual incoming player, but it does not, by itself, measure how difficult a later D acquisition would be. Two leagues with identical rosters and the same initial add could produce the same fixed-roster result even when their remaining waiver pools differ. Show a separately labeled replacement diagnostic using the observed pool, or study the symmetric future-transaction policy below; do not claim the first baseline has solved future waiver scarcity.

A later comparison may allow both paths the same explicit, non-clairvoyant continuation policy and rules, while respecting their actual remaining acquisition budgets, roster capacities and waiver restrictions. Branches may face different states because the initial plan spent a real resource; that is the consequence to model, not a reason to give one branch free extra transactions. Never assume a dropped player can be immediately reclaimed or that future competitors leave the same available players untouched. If claim success or future availability is unknown, present scenarios rather than invent probabilities; attach probabilities only with separately calibrated evidence.

At each applicable date, optimize the lineup using data available at `t`: each player-game can occupy at most one eligible slot, each slot has at most one player, and manager/provider locks, daily versus weekly windows, reserve rules and caps still apply. Use legal empty slots and benching where appropriate. Expected contributions that are already unconditional must not be multiplied by participation probability a second time. Keep goalie-start/appearance probabilities separate when their evidence and allowed use differ. The simple assignment analogy is [documented by Google](https://developers.google.com/optimization/assignment/assignment_example); full roster, timing and period constraints require more than that example.

### 4. Use replacement as an explanation, not an extra penalty

A diagnostic can compare holding player `p` with legally replacing `p` using a specific available alternative or bounded feasible set, over the same future dates and roster. Make the hypothetical acquisition time, budget and availability assumptions explicit: an option observed now is not guaranteed to remain available later. Name the replacement, usable starts and displaced players; a negative gap is possible if the replacement is actually better. This answers *why* a player may be harder to replace. It is not automatically the value of every add/drop involving `p`.

Do **not** take the already-computed whole-transaction `ΔF` and subtract a separate positional VORP, D bonus, or replacement penalty again: the optimized counterfactual already contains any scarcity and displacement it models. Likewise, do not sum independent per-position baselines where the same multi-eligible player would fill multiple slots simultaneously. A replacement reference is only as credible as its eligible pool, projections and the limited search that produced it.

### 5. Categories, ratios and goaltenders

- **Counting categories:** compare the whole lineup's native-unit deltas for each period, such as goals, assists, shots, hits and blocks, with each category's direction and league settings. For H2H, the importance of a category depends on matchup state and opponent; for roto, it depends on the standings, remaining season and game limits. Show supported gains and sacrifices. A generic Z-score sum is not automatically matchup win probability or standings value.
- **Ratios:** pool the underlying components before computing the team's ratio. For example, save percentage uses total saves / total shots faced; goals-against average uses total goals allowed × 60 / total goalie minutes, subject to league definitions. Compare the full before/after team ratios, not an unweighted average or subtraction of individual goalies' percentages. A ratio of projected component means is not necessarily the expected ratio; if the denominator or relevant components are missing or zero, the ratio is unknown. [NHL's glossary](https://www.nhl.com/info/hockey-glossary) supplies the underlying hockey definitions.
- **Goalie needs:** account for required starts versus appearances, already credited progress, eligible scheduled starts, conditional starting status and the actual penalty if the minimum is missed. Expected starts meeting a threshold do not prove the threshold will be met. Use a calibrated joint scenario model before presenting a probability of meeting it or winning categories. Otherwise present the expected/known evidence and uncertainty, with the existing manager choice about accepting risk.
- **Category tradeoffs:** keep componentwise non-dominated alternatives and no move visible where supported. A later probability or scalar objective requires an explicitly chosen scoring utility, forecast coverage and calibration; future opponents or standings may be unavailable. The basketball paper is a useful warning about variability, not an approved hockey formula.

### 6. Uncertainty and truthful presentation

Show current gain and later impact as separate values with dates, scoring units, source/forecast cutoff, continuation assumption and replacement/claim conditions. Explain a lower current gain when it avoids a larger estimated future loss, but do not label a player “safe” or a plan “optimal” merely because it ranked first in bounded search. Include no-move and, when evaluated and supported, a lower-retention-cost alternative. Distinguish a verified legal action from a conditional or illustrative scenario.

Suppress or mark future comparisons unknown when a material game, eligibility, waiver rule, candidate pool, lineup constraint, forecast use permission, goalie input or budget is missing or stale. Missing is not zero. If only a partial candidate set or selected intervals were evaluated, say so; never assert that the unseen alternative is inferior. Report horizon sensitivity and scenario ranges as **sensitivity**, not confidence or prediction intervals. Statistical intervals or claim-success probabilities require separate calibration and coverage evidence.

## Validation and implementation milestones

These are proposed, testable gates. They authorize no implementation or rollout by themselves. Establish success thresholds and the product tradeoff policy before evaluating the results; none are claimed here.

1. **Contract and input audit.** Pin the active code/leases; map current drop, best-fit, planner and stream outputs; inventory as-of available-pool, schedule, eligibility, waiver, forecast and historical coverage. Define selected and future boundaries, fixed-roster continuation, data provenance and unknown behavior. Output a coverage matrix and explicit unsupported cases.
2. **Offline points prototype.** Use synthetic, deterministic fixtures and the existing weekly scorer as the oracle. Require unchanged `ΔW`, a separate `ΔF`, legal lineup assignment, and no-move results. Exercise D-versus-F scarcity, a genuinely replaceable D, an incoming long-term upgrade, no available replacement, slot congestion/off-nights, C/LW and UTIL overlaps, reserve slots, conditional claims and failure, roster/lock timing, equal horizon boundaries and a multi-leg stream. Check that a player is never assigned to two slots, unconditional expectations are not multiplied twice, and no transaction/period is double counted.
3. **Search preservation.** Build cases where a short-term dominated plan has a better future roster, where the first two drop choices are poor, and where a stream's final held player changes the later outcome. Verify proposal generation, candidate dominance, representative selection, planner beam and stream selection keep an appropriate future-aware frontier or clearly report truncation. Demonstrate a case where a D is still the right drop. Preserve protected/out/reserve/unknown gates and no-move; compare performance to an exhaustive *small-fixture* oracle, not an unsupported claim of global optimality.
4. **Categories and goalie feasibility.** Add native-category tradeoffs and pooled-ratio cases (different shot/minute volumes, zero denominator, lower-is-better), minimum starts versus appearances, unknown starting probability and forfeiture risk. Do not ship a category scalar or probability until a manager-approved utility and calibrated scenario model exist.
5. **Historical, time-respecting shadow evaluation.** Reconstruct snapshots from archived league rosters, availability, eligibility, rules, budgets, schedules and forecast versions known at each decision cutoff; do not use today’s waiver pool or revised future information. Compare current RSO, the proposed policy, and no move on the *same* opportunities, using identical permitted information and predeclared continuation rules. Freeze decisions before seeing outcomes; replay realized starts/points or category components with consistent legal lineups and explicitly document counterfactual player-availability and manager-action assumptions. A perfect-hindsight lineup can be reported only as an unattainable upper bound, not as realized strategy performance. If historical snapshots do not exist, report that blocker rather than substituting today's data.
6. **Decision and rollout proposal.** Report paired current-window and later outcomes, decision regret against a clearly labeled feasible comparator or hindsight bound, move/no-move rates, changed recommendations and reasons, drop positions, acquisition consumption, incomplete-coverage rate and runtime/search truncation. Segment by league depth, roster settings, scoring format, position/eligibility, schedule congestion, injuries and goalie rules; use time-based holdouts and uncertainty on aggregate estimates. Check that the model does not merely avoid defense drops and does not harm the manager's stated current-period objective. Specify agreed thresholds, privacy-safe fixture evidence and a staged shadow/opt-in rollout before any recommendation behavior changes.

This evaluation estimates performance only under the replay's stated assumptions; unobserved competing-manager transactions and changed future decisions make counterfactual outcomes uncertain. Calibration of forecasts, conditional availability or statistical intervals is a separate test. No historical evaluation, production read, database write, deployment or implementation was performed for this report.
